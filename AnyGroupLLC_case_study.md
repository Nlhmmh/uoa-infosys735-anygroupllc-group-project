AWS Case Study
Case overview

Over the last 15 years, AnyGroupLLC has built a multi-billion dollar supermarket chain in North America. They were able to provide a wide range of quality products and brands not commonly available, at a price point that demonstrates value for money. They sell a variety of products, such as electronics, furniture, grocery items, and cleaning products. 

With their logistics and supply chain routes often travelling through New Zealand after expanding in Australia, AnyGroupLLC now wants to establish a base to serve New Zealand customers. New Zealand now offers an interesting value proposition for the business. Due to diverse markets, new brands and products will be sold to New Zealand customers. More importantly, AnyGroupLLC can also export New Zealand products to their established stores globally, expanding their portfolio of products offered. AnyGroupLLC is currently making a decision on whether to deploy a range of their IT systems, such as their new website application, ERP system, inventory management software, and store security in a co-location, or to go with a cloud provider. 

The business is keen to experiment and pilot some new technologies with the New Zealand business. The executive team has heard a lot around the topic of AI/ML, and is keen to explore how the technology can be used to achieve their business objectives. 

 

Interview Script

Can you please share what your business goals are?

CTO: My goals for this year will revolve around high availability, and scalability, as we are looking to grow our business in New Zealand, but ensure that our customers get access to our website when needed. I want to ensure a smooth, and great customer experience at our launch. I also want to increase productivity of our internal employees, so I am keen to hear a little more about what AWS has to offer. 

CFO: My goals this year would be to find better ways of managing our IT costs. We want to explore an appropriate model and it can be used to manage the expected demand with forecasting. In addition to this, we want to learn how we can manage the finances from an operating perspective, compared to capital expenditure. We have issues with forecasting and demand, and this is a result of the recent supply chain challenges, stock outage and resource challenges. The business had a recent issue where one of our products overperformed our expectations, but due to supply chain issues, we were unable to restock the product in time for our customers. 

CISO: My goals for this year would be to increase the security posture of the company. Previously, we released a controversial marketing campaign, which resulted in our website being subjected to DDoS attacks. We struggled to keep our website up and running, after the attacks, so I want to get a good understanding of how to build resiliency into our website, such that it is not easily taken down by a bad actor. 

How many people do you think will access your website over the year?

CTO: On average, we think we might get around 500,000 visits per day on the website, however we envision that this number will be much higher when there are sales. For example, when we run sales on special occasions such as Halloween, Matariki, Chinese New Year, etc. We are also planning on importing new products that New Zealand has previous not gotten accessed to prior, so we predict that there’s going to be more visits during that time. 

Are there any compliance regulations or security requirements that you have to comply with?

CISO: PCI compliance, as we will be offering our customers to buy products off of our website. One of my biggest worries is understanding how secure our IT infrastructure is going to be. If a malicious user gets access into our IT infrastructure and extracts large amount of high value data, that could be a reputational risk to our organization. I am interested in suggestions around aligning to industry/government standards, and how we can be compliant with these standards. 

How big is the team? What kind of skill sets does your current IT team have? 

IT Manager: We have 15 employees that will be managing and operating the new website. My team is currently spending time making sure that the website works, and putting out fires whenever there is one. For example, my team can spend weeks patching vulnerabilities, which in return, doesn’t give them a lot of time to innovate and find ways to optimize our website. I am curious to learn how moving to the cloud will help, as it will be a significant cost and effort to train and upskill my team. 

What is the technology stack you’re using to build the website?

IT Manager: Apache Web Server for the frontend, Oracle for the database, .Net core for the backend applications. Apart from the oracle database, we currently store the catalogue images of all products in another centralised server. However, we are almost reaching its capacity and we are exploring a cloud solution as an alternative. All images need to be accessible from anywhere around the world.

Are you able to provide more details on the compute resources?

IT Manager: Yes, certainly. For the frontend, we have two physical severs running on Linux systems, each with 2 CPUs and 4GB memory. The Oracle database server has 8 CPUs and 32GB memory, and 2 TB storage. The centralised server for catalogue images currently runs on a Linux system with 5TB storage. For the backend, we have four physical severs running on Windows system, each with 4 CPUs and 16GB memory.
